Takeaways of the week

  • Keep secrets out of prompts. Never paste API keys, passwords or private links into an AI tool; use a secrets manager and short-lived tokens that agents fetch when needed. Day 245
  • Plant your own canaries. Put a few fake credentials in documents and folders your AI agents can read, and set alerts so you know within minutes if one is ever used. Day 245
  • Do not read a stock's first week as a verdict. Judge AI vendors on reliability, pricing and quarterly results; first-week swings mostly reflect supply and sector mood. Day 244
  • Check that your detection works in hours, not days. Test whether an intrusion that reaches admin rights in three hours would trigger an alert someone acts on the same day. Day 244
  • Benchmark Gemini 4 Neon against your current mid-tier model. Run the same ten real tasks on Neon, GPT-6.2 Sol and your current choice, and compare cost per correct answer, not price per token. Day 243
  • Treat the thinking level as a cost dial. Start each workflow at low or medium and raise it only for the steps where accuracy measurably improves. Day 243
  • Re-run your cost model on GPT-6.2. Test whether Sol at one fifth of Astra's price is good enough for your coding and agent tasks before paying for the flagship. Day 242
  • Expect cyber refusals and apply for vetting early. If your team does legitimate penetration testing or exploit research, start OpenAI's trusted-access process now so new limits do not block your work. Day 242
  • Treat agent test environments like production. Block outbound network access by default, keep credentials out of reach and patch the tools inside the sandbox. Day 241
  • Keep answer keys away from agents. Store grading data and test answers where the agent being tested cannot reach them, so cheating is impossible rather than just forbidden. Day 241
  • Roll out AI tools in stages with an off switch. Before extending an AI tool to every site, run it in a few locations first and test that you can turn it off in minutes. Day 240
  • Separate correlation from cause in incidents. When something breaks right after a change, collect logs and a timeline before blaming or clearing the new component. Day 240
  • Treat agent extensions like code you ship. Pin versions, prefer signed extensions and review what your coding agent's plugins can run on your machine. Day 239
  • Give each partner its own narrow key. Issue a separate, least-privilege, rotatable credential to every vendor so one leak cannot reach your other systems. Day 239
