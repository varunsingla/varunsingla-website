Takeaways of the week

  • Plan for open-weight models as an option. Test an open model like Mistral Large 4 for tasks where privacy or cost matters, and compare it with a hosted model on your own data. Day 223
  • Do not depend on a single model hub. Mirror critical models and keep your pipeline portable in case hub ownership or terms change. Day 223
  • Treat every agent as a low-trust identity. Give agents short-lived, narrowly scoped credentials and require human approval for high-risk actions. Day 222
  • Never leave agent control panels exposed. Put dashboards behind authentication and a VPN, since the Cairn campaign was found through exposed panels. Day 222
  • Read the capex and free-cash-flow lines, not just the AI headline. When you judge an AI vendor's staying power, check whether spending is funded by operating cash flow or by debt. Day 221
  • Benchmark inference cost, not only model quality. Measure cost per successful task on your own workload, since serving efficiency can matter as much as the model. Day 221
  • Check your compute supplier's balance sheet. If a key GPU or cloud vendor is heavily indebted, keep a second provider or portable workloads. Day 220
  • Wait for the weights before you plan around Kimi K3.1 Pro. Check the licence and run your own tests on November 4 rather than trusting self-reported benchmarks. Day 220
  • Read the filing, not the headline. When Anthropic's S-1 is public, compare compute spend with revenue to judge how sustainable model pricing is. Day 219
  • Price your agent per task. Multiply steps by tokens at GPT-6.1 Terra's rates and compare with a human's time before automating. Day 219
  • Test agents on long tasks. Run a multi-step job and check whether the model recovers from an error and asks before risky actions. Day 218
  • Decide what an agent may remember. Write down which personal or company data persistent memory can store and how to delete it. Day 218
